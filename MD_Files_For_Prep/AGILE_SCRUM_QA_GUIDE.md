## 1. AGILE FUNDAMENTALS

### What is Agile?
Agile is an iterative approach to software development that focuses on delivering value to customers faster and with fewer headaches. It emphasizes flexibility, collaboration, and customer feedback.

### Agile Values

**From Agile Manifesto:**
- Individuals and interactions over processes and tools
- Working software over comprehensive documentation
- Customer collaboration over contract negotiation
- Responding to change over following a plan

### Agile vs Waterfall

| Aspect | Agile | Waterfall |
|--------|-------|----------|
| Approach | Iterative | Sequential |
| Flexibility | High | Low |
| Customer Involvement | Continuous | Limited |
| Documentation | Minimal | Extensive |
| Delivery | Incremental | End of project |
| Risk | Low | High |

### Agile Methodologies

**Popular Methodologies:**
- Scrum
- Kanban
- Extreme Programming (XP)
- Lean
- Feature-Driven Development (FDD)

### Interview Questions

**Q1: What is Agile?**
- Iterative software development
- Focus on customer value
- Flexible and collaborative
- Responds to change

**Q2: What are the Agile values?**
- Individuals over processes
- Working software over documentation
- Customer collaboration over contract
- Responding to change over plan

**Q3: What is the difference between Agile and Waterfall?**
- Agile: Iterative, flexible
- Waterfall: Sequential, rigid
- Agile: Continuous delivery
- Waterfall: End delivery

## 2. SCRUM FRAMEWORK

### What is Scrum?
Scrum is an Agile framework for developing, delivering, and sustaining complex products. It emphasizes teamwork, accountability, and iterative progress toward well-defined goals.

### Scrum Framework Components

**Three Pillars:**
- Transparency
- Inspection
- Adaptation

**Five Events:**
- Sprint Planning
- Daily Scrum
- Sprint Review
- Sprint Retrospective
- Sprint

**Three Roles:**
- Product Owner
- Scrum Master
- Development Team

**Three Artifacts:**
- Product Backlog
- Sprint Backlog
- Increment

### Scrum Process Flow

```
Product Backlog
    ↓
Sprint Planning
    ↓
Sprint Backlog
    ↓
Daily Scrum (Daily)
    ↓
Sprint Review (End of Sprint)
    ↓
Sprint Retrospective (End of Sprint)
    ↓
Increment (Potentially Shippable)
```

### Interview Questions

**Q1: What is Scrum?**
- Agile framework
- Teamwork and accountability
- Iterative progress
- Well-defined goals

**Q2: What are the three pillars of Scrum?**
- Transparency: Visible progress
- Inspection: Regular review
- Adaptation: Adjust based on inspection

**Q3: What are the Scrum events?**
- Sprint Planning
- Daily Scrum
- Sprint Review
- Sprint Retrospective
- Sprint

## 3. AGILE MANIFESTO

### Agile Manifesto Principles

**Four Values:**
1. Individuals and interactions over processes and tools
2. Working software over comprehensive documentation
3. Customer collaboration over contract negotiation
4. Responding to change over following a plan

### Twelve Principles

**Principles:**
1. Our highest priority is to satisfy the customer through early and continuous delivery of valuable software.
2. Welcome changing requirements, even late in development.
3. Deliver working software frequently, from a couple of weeks to a couple of months.
4. Business people and developers must work together daily.
5. Build projects around motivated individuals.
6. Face-to-face conversation is the most efficient method of communication.
7. Working software is the primary measure of progress.
8. Agile processes promote sustainable development.
9. Continuous attention to technical excellence and good design.
10. Simplicity is essential.
11. The best architectures, requirements, and designs emerge from self-organizing teams.
12. At regular intervals, the team reflects on how to become more effective.

## 6. SCRUM EVENTS

### Sprint Planning

**Purpose:**
- Plan work for the sprint
- Select items from product backlog
- Create sprint backlog
- Define sprint goal

**Duration:**
- Time-boxed to 8 hours for 2-week sprint
- Proportional for other sprint lengths

### Daily Scrum

**Purpose:**
- Sync team on progress
- Identify impediments
- Plan next 24 hours
- Inspect progress toward sprint goal

**Format:**
- 15-minute time-box
- Three questions:
  - What did I do yesterday?
  - What will I do today?
  - Do I have any impediments?

### Sprint Review

**Purpose:**
- Review increment with stakeholders
- Get feedback
- Adapt product backlog
- Celebrate success

**Duration:**
- Time-boxed to 4 hours for 2-week sprint
- Proportional for other sprint lengths

### Sprint Retrospective

**Purpose:**
- Inspect sprint
- Identify improvements
- Plan improvements
- Team collaboration

**Duration:**
- Time-boxed to 3 hours for 2-week sprint
- Proportional for other sprint lengths

### Sprint

**Duration:**
- Fixed length (1-4 weeks)
- Consistent duration
- No changes during sprint
- Deliver potentially shippable increment

### Interview Questions

**Q1: What are the Scrum events?**
- Sprint Planning
- Daily Scrum
- Sprint Review
- Sprint Retrospective
- Sprint

**Q2: What is the purpose of Daily Scrum?**
- Sync team on progress
- Identify impediments
- Plan next 24 hours
- Inspect progress

**Q3: What is the difference between Sprint Review and Retrospective?**
- Review: With stakeholders, inspect increment
- Retrospective: Team only, improve process
- Review: Product-focused
- Retrospective: Process-focused

## CONCLUSION

This comprehensive guide covers all essential Agile/Scrum for QA topics for interview preparation. Key takeaways:

1. **Agile Fundamentals**: Iterative, flexible, customer-focused, responds to change
2. **Agile Manifesto**: Individuals over processes, working software, collaboration, responding to change
3. **Agile Principles**: Customer satisfaction, welcome change, frequent delivery, collaboration
4. **Scrum Framework**: Three pillars, five events, three roles, three artifacts
5. **Scrum Roles**: Product Owner, Scrum Master, Development Team
6. **Scrum Events**: Sprint Planning, Daily Scrum, Sprint Review, Sprint Retrospective, Sprint
7. **Scrum Artifacts**: Product Backlog, Sprint Backlog, Increment
8. **Sprint Planning**: Select items, define goal, create sprint backlog
9. **Daily Standup**: Sync progress, identify impediments, plan next day
10. **Sprint Review**: Review increment, get feedback, adapt backlog
11. **Sprint Retrospective**: Inspect sprint, identify improvements, plan improvements
12. **Backlog Management**: Prioritize, refine, add/remove items
13. **User Stories**: As a... I want... So that... format
14. **Acceptance Criteria**: Given-When-Then format, testable conditions
15. **Definition of Done**: When work is complete, ensure quality
16. **Definition of Ready**: When story is ready for sprint, ensure clarity
17. **QA in Agile**: Participate in ceremonies, test continuously, ensure quality
18. **Testing in Scrum**: Test-driven, continuous testing, multiple levels
19. **Agile Testing Practices**: TDD, BDD, exploratory testing, CI
20. **Continuous Testing**: Throughout lifecycle, immediate feedback, quality gates
21. **Test Automation**: Regression, smoke, API, performance tests
22. **Agile Metrics**: Coverage, defects, velocity, burndown charts
